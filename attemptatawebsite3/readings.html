Response to: Nuclear War: The Rising Risk, and How We Stop It

W.J. Hennigan, NY Times.

The 21st century almost saw the start of possibly the World’s biggest crisis, so much that calling it a “Nightmare come True” would be an understatement. During the Russo-Ukrainian tensions starting in 2014, with Russia’s annexation of Crimea, the World as a whole was its closest to Nuclear Warfare, an event that hasn’t been so close to happening since the Cold War back in the late 20th century. After the nuclear age began, following the conclusion of World War II, The United States and Russia entered into an arms race, amassing tens of thousands of nuclear arms in a few decades. The amount of nuclear arms became drastically reduced after these two formed a better relationship, however, these have shown a slight increase, at least compared to before, in the amount since the situation between Russia and Ukraine. With threats from President Vladimir Putin of nuclear weapon usage, the fear for Nuclear War was at an alltime high, with many areas in Ukraine training doctors and nurses for possible scenarios of dealing with radiation, what this article by W.J. Hennigan tells us not only how close we were from experiencing a Nuclear War, but how living through it would be like.

When hearing the word “nuclear” many might suspect or assume that it refers to Nuclear Bombs, such used during the end of World War II, however, with advances in technology, massive amounts of destruction, while not Nuclear Bomb sized, can now be stored in tactical rounds or even missiles a fraction size of a nuclear bomb. Once one of these missiles is launched, the Nightmare begins. The explosion, reaching temperatures hotter than the surface of the sun, anything caught by it is incinerated, burnt to ashes, chances of surviving are slim to none, but there is always a chance for survival, but it doesn’t mean you would want to survive. Apart from the high temperature, the explosion would also generate a blinding light, your vision would become nonexistent, and the surrounding materials, like glass from houses, will come flying towards you, causing major injuries, if not your demise. Dirt and polluted air will get into your lungs, it will be hard to breathe, and even after all of that, the radiation still lingers on, and if it doesn’t affect you at that point, it will later on, or affect your future offsprings. All of this sounds like something out of a movie, but the reality is that it can happen at any time now, maybe in a few hours, or tomorrow or next week, with the worst part being that no one will be safe. These sorts of weapons have advanced so much that it could reach any of us, yet many still live with peace in mind, not noticing the ominous danger that lingers on today, that at any given point can occur. We aren't as prepared for a situation like this, at least not all of us, and the fact that this fear isn't more common, that more thought was put into creating these weapons than in ways to defend against them, calling it worrying would be a massive understatement.
